02

Childhood and family background

Rev Paul Yang is Chinese, and his real name is Yang Yi Lu.

The two parents are Mr Yang An Ban and Mrs Wang Shi Lan and Their children,

are the oldest of the three. He was born on1900 April 23 Morning at 05:10

am in Xiao Fu Khe quarter, Khu Min Township, Yunnan Province in China.

His father, Mr Yang An Ban, is a successful and famous

martial arts master. He also comes from a wealthy family with a descended

lineage spanning many generations.

Grandmother, Yang Shi is a successful Chinese medicine

doctor and Chinese herbalist. Mr Yang An Ban, his first son, Yang Yi Lu, aims

to continue the tradition of his parents, teach and train the famous martial

arts. The grandmother loved her grandson Yang Yi Lu and took him wherever she

went, so he was also familiar with Chinese medicine.

The heritage his parents and grandparents he received was

very useful when he later served in the mountainous region.

Father Mr Yang Ann Ban is the smart and quality of the first

son being a perfect person, he was able to stir the firstborn for his work.

Especially happy that I had a good renewal.

But God’s great plan is greater than a human plan.

03

Pursuing Education and serving the government

Yang  Yi Lu was born

in Kunming on April 23, 1900. In 1918, he studied in Kunming City to acquire

high school and was successfully passed with a good grade. Then he entered the

cadet course entrance exam in 1919. He completed the cadet/ military academy

training course for 4 years of school attendance.

At that time, it was much more difficult to enter the

military academy than it is now. In 1923, he became a major after excelling at

the training school which received a promotion.

In 1924, he was promoted from cadet to captain and then to

lieutenant colonel. Then he was transferred to the army at Guangzhou City, in Guangdong

Province, and served in the military.

In 1925, it was a difficult situation for France in the European

region and he was sent as a military officer and political adventures among those who

did not have the courage to learn, attend, study, and get involved. Captain Yang

Yi Lu, was bravely willing to serve and attend with all his might. He tried

to get involved in comparisons. After a few years of serving in France, he

was able to return successfully and proud of the country.

In 1926, the senior officer's bravery, taking risks Strong

agile Being active. Seeing the amputations in the country

He was promoted to the rank of Major, saying that he was a

promising person

Raised.

When he was promoted to the rank of Major, when he invited

the dignitaries, also invited his father martial arts master U Yang Ann Phang.

Father was very proud of the son.

In the future, his father’s heritage such as money, gold and

other treasures was very helpful to Major Yang Yi Lu’s missionary work. Even in

the presence of Father’s eyes, his honour makes Father agree due to he is holding

a position. When he asked for money, he gained the father’s trust and

interrogated him without it, money was issued easily.

04

Accepting and believing in Christ

In Guangdong

Province, China, surrounding Guangzhou City, there was a Western

missionary, an American, Rev. Morrison, of the Assembly of God (A.G.). At that

time, he was doing missionary work in China and was known as "Mar Li Xin"

in Chinese phonetics.

Because he was a Westerner, he was not given the freedom to

work freely. However, despite the difficulties, he persevered by distributing bible

verses and Words of God leaflets, praying, and personally sharing great

testimony.

One day, a young boy came running into the military compound

of Major Yang Yi lu and handed a leaflet to Major Yang Yi Lu before running

off. The leaflet dropped then soldiers picked it up and were reading it when

the major requested to see it. Upon reading, it said, "I am the way, the

truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me" (John

14:6) and "Come to me, all who labour and are heavily laden, and I will

give you rest" (Matthew 11:28).

After reading those two scripture verses, he felt deeply

touched by God and heard the divine call through them in an extraordinary way.

From the moment he read those verses, he felt as if something was missing in

his heart, and a strong desire to know more began to grow within him.

After reading the leaflet, Major Yang Yi Lu often appeared

deep in thought, with a troubled expression. He struggled to sleep and eat, and

for about a month, he remained in a state of restless contemplation.

Since the soldiers had been observing this behaviour for

some time, they started asking, "What’s going on with the Major? Did he

break up with his girlfriend? Did she send him a letter?" These teasing

questions added to his already confused feelings, and he became so frustrated

that he lashed out and even struck the soldiers in anger.

At that time, the general sentiment among the Chinese people

was that American white citizens were seen as imperialist preachers and Western

propagandists, and they were greatly hated and despised.

After reading the leaflet, Major Yang Yi Lu felt as if

something was pushing him to seek further understanding. He went to the address

provided at the bottom of the leaflet and met with a white missionary. When

Major Yang Yi Lu, dressed in his military uniform, met the missionary, the

missionary asked, "How can I help you?" The major, feeling

dissatisfied and angry, replied, "This land is our land. Why should we,

the native people, ask foreigners like you this question?" Frustrated and

filled with rage, he turned around and returned to the military camp.

After returning to the military camp, the influence of the

leaflet continued to trouble him, making it impossible for him to rest or

sleep. Eventually, dressed in civilian clothes, he returned to the missionary,

where he shared the leaflet he had received and expressed the feelings that had

been troubling him. They discussed and conversed about it.

The missionary explained John 3:16, saying, "For God so

loved the world that He gave His one and only Son, that whoever believes in Him

shall not perish but have eternal life." He shared this scripture and,

after giving an explanation, gifted him a Bible and encouraged him to read the

small New Testament.

The major, being deeply interested, studied and read

carefully, finishing the small New Testament within 3-4 days. At that time, he

had a strong desire to know more, and with a growing urge to read further, he

wondered, "Why can't I read the whole Bible?" and "Why should I

only read the four Gospels?"

After reading the four Gospels, he felt as if he had gained

something deep within his heart. He experienced a profound sense of joy, as if

something had drawn near and was constantly close to him, as if a new presence

was always nearby, comforting him.

From that time, due to his growing strong belief in

accepting Jesus Christ as the Saviour of his life, he went to confess to Pastor

Morrison and knelt to pray.

While he was praying, he saw an elderly person dressed in

pure white come and place their hand on his head. When he opened his eyes to

see who, besides Pastor Morrison, had come, he saw only the pastor and was

greatly astonished.

When he shared and asked Pastor Morrison about the matter,

the pastor simply nodded quietly without explaining anything. However, Major

Ranieri felt an incredible lightness throughout his body and was extremely

joyful. This joy came from deep within, to the point that he could not fully

describe it.

Pastor Morrison also gave him a Bible to read. From that

moment, he felt the great calling of God in his heart and a strong desire to

preach about God's magnificent salvation. His urge to testify and share this

with others grew uncontrollably. Later, the pastor explained that the elderly

person dressed in pure white who appeared during the prayer was Jesus Christ

Himself.

**Resigning from the military to take part in God's

ministry**

In late 1926, Major Yang Yee Lu requested to meet with

senior officers to submit his resignation from the military. At that time, the

senior officers responded by saying, "You are not like others. You have a

long path ahead of you. Are you underestimating your high rank at such a young

age? You have many remarkable talents. Are you out of your mind?" With

that, they rejected his resignation and sent back his application. Although

Major Yang Yi Lu had attained a high rank, he led a humble life, which made him

deeply respected and admired by his superiors.

Colleagues and subordinates admired their humble officer,

Major Yang Yi Lu, and urged him not to leave the military. However, starting

from November 17, 1926, he became increasingly unhappy in service, feeling

distant from others and repeatedly submitting applications to resign. Finally,

the senior officer said, "Well, if you can't be convinced, then go. Keep

your rank book and card with you. If one day you want to come back to work,

bring them with you." He returned the rank book and card to Major Yang Yi Lu

without formally taking them back, instructing him to keep them in the front

pocket of his uniform at all times so they would be easily accessible if

needed. From that point onward, Major Yang Yi Lu carried his rank card in his

pocket everywhere he went.

Thus, Major Yang Yi Lu left the military in December 1926.

Valuing the mission of Jesus Christ above all worldly wealth, honour, and rank,

he made the most significant decision of his life to dedicate himself entirely

to this purpose. From that moment, he felt a great sense of inner relief and

travelled to Kwantung Province, to the city of Guangzhou, where he met the

revered Pastor Ma Li Shin, who was spreading the gospel there. The pastor

welcomed him warmly and, as a special arrangement, personally taught Yang Yi Lu

the Scriptures.

Afterwards, five teachers, including Pastor Esther Zhang

(also known as Zhang Xin Yin), who later became Pastor Yang Yi Lu’s wife,

provided structured Bible teachings. Pastor Ma Li Xin submitted the expenses

for Pastor Yang Yi Lu and the other teachers to the headquarters, which then

provided the necessary support.

when Pastor Ma Li Xin petitioning for the first blessing

for Yang Yi Lu, wore a pure white robe and lifted up an elderly person’s hand,

From the moment teacher Yang Yi Lu shared this, it was known that he would

become successful. Thus, prayers were offered to support him, and he was

nurtured and guided to prosper and flourish in a good and proper way.

Therefore, Yang Yi Lu, too,

aspired to become a great leader like his original teacher, Ma Li Xin.

Following this aspiration, he prayed to shape and refine himself according to

the divine will, and he embraced the daily influence of the pure and sacred

spirit.

At that time, Preacher/

Teacher Esther Zhang had just returned to China from Hong Kong. After attending

school in Hong Kong, she had also completed Theology training school in Hong

Kong. It was during this period that she began collaborating with the missionary

Ma Li Xin (Morrison) and started actively engaging in evangelistic work.

Teacher Morrison and his wife, along with Teacher Zhang and two other

teachers, making a total of five teachers, engaged in close, day-and-night

teaching and instruction. For nearly three years, they lived, ate, and stayed

in the same place, diligently studying and learning the Word of God.

During the period of studying the Word of God,

after about two years, he began to hear the voice of God. That mysterious and

divine voice repeatedly called out, "Chong Jiang" and "Nong Jiang."

As this voice became more frequent, he sought answers but found none. Whenever

he heard the voice during prayer with his eyes closed, he would open them to

look around but saw nothing, heard nothing further, and noticed no changes. The

surroundings remained in their usual state, as though nothing extraordinary had

occurred.

When he inquired about this with Teacher Morrison and others, they, too,

were unsure and could not provide an answer. Teacher Morrison replied, “It may

be a revelation from the Holy Spirit.” Later, he began to hear "Chong Jiang"

and "Nong Jiang" repeatedly. This prompted further investigation by

consulting local experts on Chinese geography and historians familiar with

ancient studies. They discovered that these names referred to two distinct

places.

"Chong Jiang" was the name of a region near the Myanmar-China

border, while "Nong Jiang" referred to a location near the Chinese

provincial border. Additionally, "Nong Jiang" was the name of a

river. The term "Non" was understood as "name" in the Lisu

dialect, and "Jiang" was interpreted as "river" in Chinese.

When the information about the names "Chong Jiang" and "Nong

Jiang" was confirmed, Teacher Yang Yi Lu was deeply moved and overjoyed.

He prayed daily, seeking further guidance and revelations from the Holy Spirit.

Hearing the names of these two places, linked to the voice of God, filled him

with readiness and enthusiasm to embark on a journey to those regions. He

waited expectantly, both spiritually and physically prepared, through prayer. However,

Teacher Morrison advised him with caution, saying, "Your spiritual

foundation is still young. Let us continue to pray and wait for God's

timing." This guidance encouraged patience and reliance on divine timing,

delaying his immediate ready plans to travel to those regions.

Subsequently, the voice he often heard became more frequent, and he

increasingly felt the Holy Spirit’s awakening and encouragement. With this

growing conviction, Teacher Yang Yi Lu made a firm decision to go and began

planning for the mission. At that time, his close mentors, including Teacher Ma

Li Xin, along with the group of five teachers, officially gave him and anointed

him the Christian name "Paul Yang" through prayer and a commissioning

ceremony. This marked a significant spiritual milestone as he prepared to

embark on his mission.

Around 1926, he began his journey from China

to Myanmar, traveling to the regions of Chong Jiang and Nong Jiang. Before reaching Nong Jiang, he first arrived in the

region of Chong Jiang (also called Weixi).

In that area, there were three prominent

Christian groups present:

1. **Assemblies of God (A.G.)** led by a senior pastor and his family.
2. **Church of Christ (C.C.)**, represented by Pastor Yawbi and his family.
3. **Song Ministry**, which included families involved in hymn ministry: Ah du Ma Mar (in Lisu ethnic), Ah cha Ma Mar (in Lisu ethnic), Ah yi da Ma Mar (in Lisu ethnic) , and Ah zi da Ma Mar (in Lisu ethnic).

The three groups were residing in the Can jiang (Weixi) region at that

time, awaiting the opportunity to begin their mission to the Nong region in

Myanmar. Teacher Paul Yang, together with these three groups, prayed daily for

the success of their missionary journey to Nong jiang. Filled with great joy

and enthusiasm, Missionary Paul Yang joined them in united prayer, preparing

for their mission with a shared sense of purpose and collaboration. Their

collective prayers and planning strengthened their resolve for the work ahead.

Through the experiences encountered with the pastor from the Assemblies of

God (A.G.) denomination, Teacher Paul Yang witnessed God’s unique revelations.

He experienced the painful reality of racial discrimination through the actions

of the pastor, which left him deeply shocked and disheartened. Despite sharing

the same faith, the pastor, who was of American descent, treated him

differently because of his Chinese heritage. They were not allowed to eat

together at the same table, and Teacher Paul Yang was forced to eat only the

leftovers from the pastor’s family. This experience of being treated as

inferior based on race left him with a sense of emotional weakness and

distress, further deepening his understanding of the challenges of prejudice

and discrimination.

Pastor Paul Yang, born into a wealthy and respected family with a high

social standing, and having himself been a military officer with a notable

rank, found it deeply unsettling to face such discrimination. His background,

filled with Honour and privilege, made the experience of racial prejudice all

the more painful and confusing.

However, the leaders from the Church of Christ and the Song Ministry

encouraged him to focus on the shared mission of spreading the gospel and urged

him to collaborate with them. They emphasized the unity of the work and invited

him to join forces.

Despite the internal conflict he felt, Teacher Paul Yang often considered

joining them in their efforts. But whenever he contemplated it, he felt a deep

sense of restraint, as if something was holding him back. It felt as though he

was being told, "Stay where you are. Continue the work that has been

revealed to you, as it is." This inner sense of guidance made him realize

that his path was meant to follow the direction God had already shown him, and

he needed to remain faithful to it.

No matter what, he remained steadfast in his current location, continuously

praying and seeking wisdom and knowledge from God. Eventually, he reached the

regions of Nong Jiang (also known as Nong Mi region), Fu Kung (or Shan Fa), and

Sha Fa Province.

In these areas, in order to obtain permission to preach, he needed to

formally meet with the mountain official, submit a request, and engage in

discussions. Only after these steps could he obtain an official permit to

preach. However, gaining an audience with the mountain official and obtaining

entry permission was exceedingly difficult.

The office of the mountain official was

located in a compound enclosed by stone walls, with no other entrances except

for a single entry gate, there was no other way to enter. This sole entry point

was heavily guarded with stringent security measures, making it extremely

challenging to gain access to the mountain official.

However, the plans of God are truly Marvelous

and awe-inspiring. Reflecting on the preparations God had arranged long before,

one cannot help but praise, glorify and give thanks for His grace and goodness. Hallelujah, Amen.

To provide some context and briefly revisit

the background, Reverend Paul Yang made a firm decision to leave behind his

prestigious position as a military officer to follow Christ. When he submitted

his resignation to his superior officer, along with his highly valuable

honorary card, the superior was deeply moved and reluctant to let him go but

ultimately granted permission.

At that time, the superior returned the

honorary card to him, saying,

"Do you not value this rank of officer that you achieved at such a young

age with such great effort and capability? This card is not something easily

obtained by ordinary people. Treasure it. If you wish to step away from the

job, I will grant your request, but take this honorary card with you wherever

you go. This means that you still hold this title and rank. Use it whenever

necessary."

With these words, the superior respectfully

handed back the highly significant card under the providence of God. The card

included detailed information such as his name, photograph, rank, title, and

insignia.

The rank identification card would soon prove to be of great value in

gaining access to the official the group needed to meet. Previously, the

superior officer had instructed that this rank card should always be kept in

the front pocket of one’s jacket and carried everywhere.

Thus, with this card in hand, Colonel Paul Yang, who was now also serving as

a missionary, approached the office of the official. Upon arrival, he

encountered a guard stationed at the main entrance. When he requested entry, he

was firmly denied access.

At this moment, Colonel Paul Yang presented his rank identification card.

The guards, upon seeing it, were struck with fear and immediately apologized,

granting him entry without hesitation. They were visibly shaken, trembling in

fear. One of the guards even ran inside to inform the official that a Colonel

from mainland China, Colonel Yang, had arrived to see them.

Missionary Paul Yang was granted the opportunity to meet with the governor

and was invited to move into the governor’s residence. He was hosted with meals

personally prepared by the governor’s wife.

Missionary Paul

Yang was granted the privilege of staying for three nights at the governor's

residence. During this time, he had the opportunity to discuss and coordinate future

plans with the governor, as well as speak and make requests to him. The

governor suggested that these matters be formalized through written

applications, and in response, Colonel Yang took the time to prepare and submit

the necessary application for permission to proceed with the required actions.

Six months later, a response was received

regarding Colonel Paul Yang's application. The letter confirmed that the

governor had officially granted him permission to engage in religious ministry.

At that time, it was a period when individuals with a lighter complexion (those

with pale skin, red or white faces and eyes) were heavily restricted and not

allowed entry. As a result, it was extremely difficult for missionaries from

Western countries to gain access to Myanmar.

Thus, due to God's grace,

Colonel Paul Yang was granted the opportunity to begin his ministry in the

border region, alongside other groups that had been waiting to engage in

religious work. Around 1929, good news was received

regarding the granting of permission for Christian ministry. American missionaries

from the Church of Christ, including Reverend Yaw bi and his wife, as well as

the Song Ministry group, including the couple Ah yi da Mapa and Ah si da Mama,

were granted permission to engage in ministry as well. Upon hearing this,

Reverend Paul Yang and the two missionaries’ groups, overwhelmed with joy, shed

tears and wept with gratitude, hugs each other and acknowledging God's

wonderful plan. He praised and thanked God for His grace.

After Reverend Paul Yang received the Christian

preaching permission, he extended the invitation to other groups such as the

Church of Christ and the Song Ministry group. He said, "This permission is

for all of us. We will all pray together and work together as one," and

they proceeded to pray together and collaborate in their ministry.

As a result, permission was granted to officially begin the ministry along

the “Nong Jiang”

(also known as Nong mi Law) along the river. Reverend Yaw bi from the Church of

Christ ministry took responsibility for the upper part of the river (Gong Shan

region). Reverend Paul Yang from Assemblies of God (AG) ministry took charge of

the central part (Fu kong or Sha Fa), and the lower part (Bi Jiang) was managed

by Ah Yi Da Mapa (pronounce by using the local Lisu

Ethnic dialogue) along with Ah cha Marma (pronounce by using the local Lisu

Ethnic dialogue) and Ah du Marma (pronounce by using the local Lisu Ethnic

dialogue)  from the Song ministry.

Reverend Paul Yang's presence in the central region was intended to address

and manage any issues that might arise from both the upper and lower parts of

the area. He currently holds the rank of Colonel and, being of Chinese descent,

is recognized as a significant figure within the religious community, having

been appointed by God's remarkable plan. God's plan is beyond human

comprehension. Praise be to God, Amen.

After six months, upon receiving official permission to preach and practice

his faith, Christianity gained momentum and flourished throughout the region.

Each day, many people were converted and embraced Christianity, marking the

beginning of God's great work.

Paul Yang's missionary work in Myanmar is an inspiring story of dedication

and faith. His commitment to spreading the Gospel among the Lisu people is

evident in his quick mastery of the Lisu language and literature. This allowed

him to connect deeply with the community and establish a solid foundation for

his evangelical mission. He spent most of his time traveling from house to

house and village to village, tirelessly preaching and serving.

During this period, Paul Yang was instrumental in founding Assemblies of God

churches in the region. His efforts laid the groundwork for a thriving network

of congregations that continue to grow to this day. His approach blended

cultural sensitivity with a clear focus on his spiritual mission, which

resonated with the Lisu and other ethnic groups in Myanmar.

This phase of his life also marked the beginning of the formal establishment

of Assemblies of God congregations in Myanmar, many of which trace their roots

back to Yang's efforts. His work has had a lasting impact, influencing

generations of believers and church leaders in the country.

For more detailed accounts of Paul Yang’s contributions and the growth of

Assemblies of God in Myanmar, it would be helpful to consult church archives,

biographies, or regional Pentecostal history records.

During this time, a severe cholera epidemic spread across the region,

resulting in the deaths of numerous people daily. The outbreak was extremely

terrifying, with the death rate soaring rapidly, leaving the local population

in a state of fear, panic, and hopelessness.

The cholera pathogen caused severe stomach ailments, progressing rapidly to

death within a very short period. The disease was highly contagious and aggressive,

leaving little to no time for treatment before fatal outcomes occurred. The

community faced a dire situation, as the swift and deadly nature of the

outbreak devastated families and entire villages.

(Cholera, is a

highly infectious bacterial disease caused by the bacterium *Vibrio

cholerae*. It primarily spreads through contaminated water and food.

Symptoms include severe diarrhea, dehydration, and, if untreated, can lead to

death within hours due to rapid fluid loss.

Historically, cholera outbreaks have devastated communities worldwide,

especially in regions with poor sanitation and limited access to clean drinking

water. The disease spreads rapidly in crowded or disaster-affected areas,

exacerbating health crises.)

The Missionary Paul Yang fearlessly risked his life, entering homes and

visiting one person after another who was suffering from illness. He prayed

fervently for the sick, and many people experienced healing and deliverance

from the brink of death. These miraculous moments left a profound impact on

those who witnessed them, fostering faith in Christ among not only the

recovered individuals but also their families and other eyewitnesses.

Before finishing prayers for one person, he would immediately proceed to

another, tirelessly ministering to the afflicted. The widespread healings led

to a significant number of people, including entire families, turning toward

Christ and becoming believers. These acts were seen as divine interventions,

highlighting God’s wondrous works and affirming the Missionary Paul Yang's

mission.

In some instances, the number of people seeking prayer was so overwhelming

that the elder (Paul Yang) adapted by simply touching the crowd with his hand

and proclaiming, *"In the name of the Lord Jesus, you

are healed!"* These swift prayers were made to address the

urgent needs of the masses.

During the day, he worked under the sunlight, and at night, he used bamboo

torches as makeshift lamps to travel from one location to another. He

tirelessly offered prayers and ministered to those afflicted, bringing relief

from illnesses and ailments, demonstrating his unwavering dedication to his

mission.

In the region, entire villages witnessed

Christ and became devoted followers, leading to a rapid increase in Christian

believers day by day. Over time, not only were prayers offered individually but

also in groups, resulting in a significant expansion and strengthening of the

Christian faith. The number of believers in the area grew remarkably, with

daily conversions becoming a testament to the transformative power of the

ministry.

Recognizing the extraordinary growth, Missionary Paul Yang communicated with his senior  leader, Ma Li Xin (Morrison) back in his home

region of China, sharing details about the current situation and seeking

further guidance and support for the flourishing Christian movement in the

area.

The senior leader, Ma Li Xin, advised  Missionary Paul Yang to return to his

hometown and take time to consider his marital life. The advice was rooted in

the belief that leading the growing number of believers would be more effective

with a life partner. It was suggested that working together with a spouse in

ministry would allow for shared responsibilities and provide greater strength

and stability in shepherding the rapidly expanding Christian community.

After Paul Yang returned to his hometown in

China, the senior leader, Ma Li Xin (Morrison), arranged for him to marry

Esther Zhang, a member of the previous leader and mentor' group. Initially,

Paul Yang did not immediately accept the proposal, stating he needed time to

consider the matter. Similarly, Esther Zhang was reluctant, expressing that she

was not ready for marriage. Both parties declined the arrangement multiple times,

with Esther sharing her feelings openly.

Despite their hesitations, leader Ma Li Xin

(Morrison) insisted that the union was part of God’s plan and encouraged them

to proceed. Ultimately, Paul Yang and Esther Zhang were united in marriage

through a prayer ceremony officiated by the senior leaders. Paul Yang was not

given the option to choose his partner, nor was he allowed to refuse the

decision, as it was framed as fulfilling God’s will.

The day after Paul Yang and Esther Zhang were

married, senior leader Ma Li Xin

(Morrison) sent them on their first missionary journey. Esther, who had not

prepared any belongings such as clothes or personal items, expressed her

concerns about being unprepared. Senior leader Ma Li Xin responded by saying, *"You

can arrange what you need when you arrive at your destination."*

From that moment, Esther Zhang embarked on a

life of extreme hardship, enduring challenging, dangerous, and remote areas to

fulfill her missionary duties. Her life as a missionary was marked by

relentless physical and emotional struggles. Despite these difficulties, she

dedicated her entire life to the ministry until her eventual passing and return

to the Lord’s house.

After arriving at their destination, Paul Yang and his wife

Esther Zhang settled in the "Lawoo"

village in the “Shan Fa” area. They built a home and began

their missionary work together in the region. Esther started learning the local

Lisu language and studying Lisu alphabets. She then translated hymn songs into

Lisu and organized Lisu literacy programs for the locals. Esther began teaching

Lisu alphabets, using both the local language and the alphabets widely. As a

result, many Lisu people learned to read and write.

From that time onwards, Esther Zhang began to train new

converts as teachers and send them to the churches they had established. She

also played a key role in starting new churches, continuing the work that Paul

Yang and she had initiated. These efforts contributed to the expansion of the

church and its community across the region.

Soon after, a group of individuals who opposed

the couple broke into their home, setting it on fire. Despite the constant

threats and risks from those who wished them harm, the couple faced many

challenging moments. Concerned locals feared for their lives, but the couple,

although discouraged, prayed and sought strength in Christ, ultimately deciding

to persist in their mission. Their determination to continue their work in

faith remained unshaken despite the difficulties they faced.

Afterward, Paul decided to return to their homeland to seek

financial assistance and support from his family and relatives. He managed to

gather money, help, and resources from his family, including their parents and

grandparents. Following this, they relocated to the "Goo Chay" area on the

opposite side of the "Nong Jiang" river in the "Shan Fa"

region. This marked a new chapter in their journey as they continued their

mission.

In the "Goo Chay" region, Paul and Esther Yang

built a new house and settled there. Over time, new believers continued to grow

in number each day. Esther Zhang, the female leader, conducted training

sessions for new believers, and as a result, many new congregations were

established. She began to train new converts as preachers

and send them to the churches they had established. The number of believers

continued to rise steadily throughout the entire "Shan Fa" region,

eventually filling the entire area with followers. This marked a period of

significant growth and expansion for the Christian community in the region.

The ability to increase the number of believers, establish

training for new preachers, and build congregations by sending out individuals

to various regions is a remarkable feat of leadership, which can only be

attributed to guidance from Christ. All these efforts were made possible

through the blessings and inheritance from the parents and grandparents of

Pastor Paul Yang. He and his wife were responsible for the well-being of the

trainees and new preachers, offering them shelter, food, and support as they

carried out their duties. This model of leadership and community building

played a crucial role in the expansion of their mission.

The Reverend and his wife made great

sacrifices and endured numerous hardships in order to serve the people of that

region. Before beginning their ministry, they devoted themselves to learning

the local language and personally studied the basic literacy text known as the

“Mari Mi”, the Lisu primer used as the foundation for reading and writing.

While they were learning the Lisu language and

literature themselves, they also trained many local men and women to become

teachers, as mentioned earlier. They taught them the basic Lisu literacy primer

and nurtured them through the Lisu Bible, providing continuous instruction,

discipleship, and spiritual training. Day and night, they devoted themselves to

this work, raising up and commissioning many teachers and Christian workers for

the ministry.

Around the year 193.., the Chinese government

became increasingly authoritarian and severely oppressed the Lisu people, who

were and ethnic minority.

Unable to endure the oppression any longer,

the Lisu people began to revolt. The Chinese government’s harsh policies and

repression placed an unbearable burden on the local people. As tensions

escalated, a group of Lisu people organized themselves and killed the Chinese

district governor, “Shi Wu Yee”, along with several other government officials

in the area.

News of this serious ethnic conflict soon

reached the central government in mainland China. In response, the central

government issued a special order and immediately dispatched 300 soldiers to

the region with specific instructions to deal with the situation.

At that time, the Lisu people urgently placed

with the Reverend and his wife to leave the area at once and return to their

homeland for their own safety.

Because the region had become unstable and

dangerous, remaining there meant that their lives could be in danger at any

moment. The Lisu people were also unable to protect or safeguard the Reverend

and his wife in any way.

However, the Reverend and his wife strongly

rejected and objected to the Lisu people’s advice. They firmly decided that

they would not leave the area and would continue to there and carry on with

their ministry. They remained determined to stay and continue serving despite

the circumstances.

However, there were several circumstances that

eventually led them to reconsider. Their first daughter was only five months

old, and they also realised that if they had to flee suddenly, their presence

could become and additional burden and responsibility for the Lisu people who

were already facing great difficulties. For these and other reasons, they

eventually made the difficult decision to leave the region.

After leaving the area, they continued their

journey towards mainland China. As they travelled through the mountains and

forests, they suddenly spotted, through binoculars, a large government military

force, including approximately 300 soldiers, approaching from the direction of

mainland China.

The Reverend then instructed his wife and

their five-months old daughter to hide inside a rocky cave together with the

two companions who were travelling with them. He then turned towards the

direction from which the government troops were approaching and walked out

towards that road alone.

Then, right there in the middle of the road,

he knelt down, raised his hands, and firmly resolved to explain and speak about

what had happened. Just as described earlier, he raised his hands and began

calling out loudly, explaining the situation so that the government troops in

the distance could hear him.

It was truly not an easy decision to make. The

consequences of such a decision could have resulted in unimaginable danger for

the Reverend and his entire family. The central government was extremely angry

and enraged because of the ongoing conflict involving the Lisu people. Orders

had already been given to punish the Lisu people and even to eradicate them

completely. Therefore, by intervening in such a situation, the Reverend had

very little chance of escaping safely.

Considering the circumstances, the Reverend

really should not have intervened.

The colonel leading the government troops was

a close friend and long-time colleague of Reverend Paul Yang. He had also only

recently been promoted to the position of commanding officer. His name was

Colonel Lee Zhan Jun (Lee Zhan Jun).

As Colonel Lee Zhan Jun, who was leading the

large military force from mainland China, looked through his binoculars from a

considerable distance, he suddenly recognised his old friend, Colonel Yang Yi

Lu, Kneeling on the ground, waving his hands and pleading with the soldiers not

to shoot.

Colonel Lee was utterly astonished and

bewildered. He never expected to encounter his old friend in such an unexpected

place, nor did he expect to see him in such an unexpected and extraordinary

situation.

The harsh military order carried by the

government army of 300 soldiers was an order to kill all the Lisu people living

in that region. If this order could not be stopped, there was a very real

possibility that the Lisu people would face complete annihilation.

The order reportedly stated that even if a dog

was found, it was to be killed on the spot. This meant that the intention was

to completely wipe out the Lisu population and destroy the Lisu community in

that region.

This was an order issued by the Chinese

government in anger and fury over the actions of the Lisu people.

When the colonel saw Colonel Yang Yi Lu

through his binoculars from a great distance, he was extremely astonished. When

he finally reached him, he asked him many questions, such, “How did you end up

here? What happened? Why are you here in this place and in this situation?”

Reverend Paul Yang immediately explained what

he needed to say without wasting any time. He briefly and urgently explained

that it would be best to stop the matter immediately and not proceed any

further. He explained that the Lisu people had done nothing wrong and that the

situation had arisen because of the actions of some Chinese people.

He also explained that the Lisu people had

poisoned the rivers and streams, leaving the government troops without safe

drinking water and potentially putting their lives at risk. He further

explained that the poison used on the Lisu people’s arrows was extremely

potent. When an arrow was fired, the poison could spread into the surrounding

area, and even people nearby could potentially be poisoned by the toxic powder.

He desperately tried to explain these circumstances and persuade the military

commander to reconsider, risking his own life without hesitation.

Reverend Paul Yang was willing to sacrifice

his own life in an attempt to stop, as far as he possibly could, the brutal

military order issued by the Chinese authorities to “kill everyone encountered”

in the conflict between the two ethnic groups.

Through his courageous intervention, he was

able to help bring peace to the conflict between the two groups and, most

importantly, save the Lisu people from possible annihilation.

It was truly no easy task to prevent a

conflict that had generated such deep hatred and hostility. To stand between

two groups that were on the verge of fighting, raise one’s hands, and attempt

to mediate and reconcile them was and extraordinarily dangerous act.

Either one side or the other could have shot

and killed him. It was only because of the constant care, dedication, and

prayers of the senior pastor and his wife that they were able to experience

God’s deliverance and be saved.

After that, the senior pastor and his wife

devoted themselves even more actively than before to building and expanding the

church together with believers, pastors, and teachers.

In 1931 (1935), they once again began working

in a new mission field, travelling to “Chi La Ti” Village in Upper Myanmar. To

continue their missionary journey, they had to cross the “Shi Kaw” mountain

pass. In order to reach that place, they had no choice but to cross a great ice-covered

mountain. Crossing it during the winter season was extremely difficult. It was

only through their strong determination to continue the journey regardless of

the danger to their lives that they were able to overcome the mountain and

finally reach their destination.

While the senior pastor was serving in the

rank of Major, he had been given a compass. When he resigned from his position,

he returned the compass. However, his superior allowed him to keep and use it

wherever it was needed and gave it back to him. As a result, the compass proved

extremely useful when they were crossing the great ice-covered mountain.

It took them more than three days of travel to

reach “Chi La Ti” Village. From that time onward, they once again began their

ministry in a new place and a new region.

For that first evening, the senior pastor

prayed for one person-one soul-to come and hear the message. He then began preaching.

However, many people came that evening, filling the entire house. The gathering

became so crowded with people who had come to listen to the Word of God. That

night, ten people accepted Christ.

The

Pastor proclaimed clearly and fluently in the Lisu language: “I will show you

the way to the Kingdom of Heaven. Those who want to know, listen carefully.”

Because these were village communities where

people were closely connected with one another. As people heard about the

message, large numbers gathered together to listen.

The Gospel ministry continued from village to

village and from one are to another, beginning in the Kaung Lang Phuu region

and extending as far as Amat Kyung region. Gradually, the Gospel spread

throughout upper Myanmar, reaching the Putao region, and the number of

believers continued to grow.

Through the wonderful way God used and gifted

Esther Zhang, she provided local education and training to the people in those

regions. She taught them songs and melodies, nurtured and encouraged them,

provide ongoing training and development for teachers, and sent them out to

serve in different places.

As these trained believers became involved in

various aspects of Gospel ministry contributing, supporting, and working

together the ministry became increasingly effective and widespread.

In this way, the Gospel came to fill the

entire Amat Kyung region and continued to penetrate and reach as far as the

Putao region.

In December 1942, they became well know for

baptising more than 200 people in a single day at a river in Atu La Village, in

Khaung Lang Phuu region. It was regarded as one of the most remarkable

occasions in which such a large number of people were baptised in one day.

After this, the Rev Yang and Rev Zhang

continued to devote their lives to ministry. They travelled back across the

ice-covered mountains to the Nong Mi region, continued establishing churches,

and later returned to Myanmar to conduct refresher and strengthening courses

for pastors and teachers. They also spent considerable time preparing for the

establishment of new churches and travelled to churches that had already been

established in different places, visiting them, encouraging the believers, and

preaching messages of encouragement.

From around the 19…s onward, they closely

cared for a church in Ma Mu Let Village, in the Putao area. Later, they settled

permanently in that village.

After making Ma Mu Let Village their permanent

home, they continued to visit different churches, oversee and care for them,

and encourage the pastors and believers, faithfully carrying on this ministry.

18 January 1972

Rev. Esther Zhang travelled from Ma Mu Let to

Myitkyina to conduct a refresher training course for pastors and teachers at

the Evangelical Theological College (Myitkyina, That Gone Bible School).

One morning, while she was teaching a class,

she was called home by Christ. While still teaching, she answered the LORD’s

call and peacefully returned to her heavenly Father’s home.

Throughout her entire life, Rev. Esther Zhang

served Christ faithfully, enduring great hardship, sacrifice, and difficulties

for the sake of the Gospel. Until her final breath, she remained a faithful

servant of Christ and joyfully and honourable completed her earthly race,

entering into the presence of her Lord.

After Rev. Esther Zhang was called home to be

with the Lord, Rev. Paul Yang moved permanently from Ma Mu Let to the Airport

Village in Putao.

1992

Rev. Paul Yang also faithfully carried out the

great work entrusted to him by God throughout his entire life. He lived at the

home of his daughter, Kyi Le Yang, in Airport Village, Putao. As a faithful

servant of God, he joyfully and honourably completed his earthly journey and,

until his final breath, remained a faithful servant of Christ before entering

the presence of his heavenly Father.

May God’s peace and grace rest upon and remain

with each and every reader. Amen.

In the love of our Lord Jesus Christ,

**Commemoration of the 90th Anniversary of the Assemblies of God

(AG) in Myanmar – 20 July 2021**

On 20 July 1931, the Gospel was first brought

into Myanmar through the China–Myanmar border by **Rev. Paul Yang (Yang Yi Lu)**, originally from Yunnan, China, and **Rev. Esther Zhang (Zhang Xing

Ying)**, originally from Hong Kong.

Guided by the vision of the Holy Spirit, their missionary work laid the

foundation for what would become the Assemblies of God (AG) in Myanmar.
